question: "Does meditation change the body?"

meta_description: "How meditation affects the body: studies on immune function, inflammation, telomeres and telomerase, and cellular aging."
theme_description: |
  Beyond the mind, meditation can leave measurable traces in the body. This theme gathers research on how contemplative practices affect immune markers, inflammation, telomerase activity, and sleep. Together, they suggest that the health benefits of meditation may be rooted not just in psychology, but in physiology.
research_summary: |
  **The evidence points to measurable effects on the body, though most studies are small.** In a randomized trial, [Davidson et al. (2003)](/brain-and-immune-function/) found that employees who took an 8-week MBSR course produced more antibodies after a flu vaccine than a wait-list group. Two studies link meditation to telomere biology, which is tied to cellular aging: [Jacobs et al. (2011)](/immune-telemorase-psychological-mediators/) found higher telomerase activity after a three-month retreat, linked to gains in purpose in life and perceived control, and [Dasanayaka et al. (2023)](/telomere-dynamics/) found that long-term meditators had telomeres about 32% longer than matched non-meditators.

  Other studies connect body and mind. [Shields et al. (2020)](/deconstructing-concentration-interference-control/) found that retreat participants with higher inflammation had weaker attention control, and [Lutz et al. (2013)](/activation-during-anticipation-pain/) found that expert meditators rated pain as less unpleasant, though no less intense, than novices. A randomized trial also found that a three-day mindfulness retreat, unlike a matched relaxation retreat, strengthened a resting connection between the default mode network and a region involved in self-regulation, and that change statistically accounted for part of a drop in the inflammation marker IL-6 four months later ([Creswell et al., 2016](/mindfulness-connectivity-inflammation/)). Against a health program matched to MBSR in format, teaching and home practice, an 8-week MBSR course eased distress and the cortisol response to a stress test no more than the health program did, but a nerve-driven skin flare provoked during the test grew less after MBSR. Inflammatory proteins in the skin didn't differ, and the flare difference was gone four months later ([Rosenkranz et al., 2013](/mbsr-active-control-skin-inflammation/)). Even five days can register: students randomly assigned to 20 minutes a day of integrative body–mind training showed a smaller rise in the stress hormone cortisol around a mental arithmetic test, and higher levels of secretory IgA, an antibody in saliva, than students given relaxation training, though these were measured only after training ([Tang et al., 2007](/five-days-meditation-attention-stress/)).

  The main caveat is design: several of these studies compare long-term meditators with non-meditators at a single point in time, which shows an association rather than proving that meditation caused the difference.
