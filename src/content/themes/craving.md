theme_description: |
  Buddhist teaching places craving at the root of suffering. The Pali word, *taṇhā*, literally means "thirst": the restless pull toward what we want and away from what we don't. Much meditation practice trains people to notice that pull as it arises, without automatically acting on it. Modern addiction treatment has borrowed the idea. Programs such as mindfulness-based relapse prevention teach "urge surfing," riding out a craving like a wave that rises, peaks and passes.

  This theme gathers studies that test whether mindfulness helps people with addictions and cravings: randomized trials for smoking, for alcohol and drug use after treatment, and for opioid misuse alongside chronic pain; analyses of how mindfulness might work, for example by weakening the link between craving and use; and research on food cravings.
