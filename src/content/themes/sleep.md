theme_description: |
  Meditation has long been linked to better rest, but how exactly does it affect the sleeping brain and body? This theme brings together studies exploring how contemplative practice influences sleep quality, architecture, and related physiological processes. From improving subjective sleep satisfaction to altering inflammatory markers and cognitive control mechanisms, these findings show how meditation might help us fall asleep faster, sleep more deeply, and wake more refreshed. They also hint at something deeper: that our waking practices shape our sleeping mind.
research_summary: |
  **Mindfulness meditation improves sleep in randomized trials, especially how people experience their sleep.** A meta-analysis of 18 randomized trials in adults with sleep problems found that mindfulness programs improved self-reported sleep quality more than time-matched classes, with a small effect after the programs and a medium one 5–12 months later, but showed no clear difference from established treatments such as CBT-I ([Rusch et al., 2019](/mindfulness-sleep-meta-analysis/)). In older adults with moderate sleep problems, a six-week mindfulness course improved sleep quality more than a matched sleep hygiene course, with a large effect size, and also reduced fatigue and depression ([Black et al., 2015](/meditation-sleep-older-adults/)). In adults with chronic insomnia, both MBSR and a mindfulness program combined with behavioral sleep techniques (MBTI) cut time spent awake at night by about 44 minutes, and MBTI produced the strongest long-term reduction in insomnia severity ([Ong et al., 2014](/mindfulness-chronic-insomnia/)).

  Objective measures are more modest: in the insomnia trial, sleep recordings changed less than people's own reports. Studies of long-term practitioners hint at deeper effects, though. Vipassana meditators had more slow wave and REM sleep than non-meditators across age groups, and kept more deep sleep into their 50s ([Pattanashetty et al., 2010](/vipassana-enhanced-sleep/)), but that comparison can't rule out other lifestyle differences.
